01 the problem

One campaign. Ten tools. Zero flow.

Picture a marketer at Intuit about to launch a personalized campaign. They start with a brief. Then they wait on content SLAs. Chase targeting teams. Coordinate experimentation. Go back to content for revisions. Then approvals. Then activation. Across 10+ tools and 5+ teams.

That is one campaign. It takes 1 to 2 weeks. And a marketer might run dozens a month. Multiply it across every product Intuit markets. That is the problem.

02 the vision

Tell me what you need. I'll handle the rest.

Marketing Studio was built on one idea: what if every marketer had a single place to go, and AI did the heavy lifting across every step of the campaign workflow?

It replaces a fragmented ecosystem of tools and teams with a single agentic AI layer. Redundant tools get eliminated. Team dependencies shrink. Marketers stop coordinating and start creating. The homepage became the entry point: natural language intent in, agent-built next moves out.

03 the challenge

Designing for something that did not exist yet

No UX playbook. No precedent inside Intuit. No obvious reference points outside it. The challenge was not visual. It was conceptual.

  1. q1

    How do you show AI progress without losing trust?

    Marketers are accountable for every campaign. They needed visibility into what the AI was doing and why, without being overwhelmed.

  2. q2

    How do you structure a workspace for non-deterministic workflows?

    Agentic workflows are dynamic. The AI decides the path. The design had to accommodate that while still feeling coherent and learnable.

  3. q3

    How do you decide what belongs in the product?

    With AI, almost anything feels buildable. Feature decisions had to be grounded in user need, not engineering possibility.

04 early research

We looked wide before we looked deep

We studied AI tools across categories: conversational AI like ChatGPT and Gemini, agentic tools like Cursor and Replit, workspace tools like Confluence, creative tools like Napkin AI. Three patterns shaped our direction.

output centricity

The artifact, not the conversation, is the primary focus.

progressive transparency

Show enough for trust, not everything under the hood.

guided autonomy

Clear decision points, no micromanagement.

05 the ia framework

One mental model. Every workflow.

Before designing any screen, we had to answer a more fundamental question: how is this product even structured? The answer had to work across every workflow, every user type, and every future capability we had not built yet. I defined five organizing pillars. Every interaction in the platform maps to one or more of them.

A set of How Might We questions grounded every design decision: visibility into what the AI is doing and why; workflows that are dynamic without feeling unpredictable; centralizing tools to reduce fragmentation; supporting multiple workflow types without multiplying complexity.

06 the pattern language

A pattern for every interaction

With no existing patterns to follow, we built our own. Every interaction in Marketing Studio maps to a pattern. Each pattern serves one or more of the five pillars, giving the platform a consistent, scalable foundation across every workflow, present or future. It became the UX vocabulary for agentic AI at Intuit.

07 research and validation

Designing with evidence, not assumption

UX issues were categorized using JJ Garrett's five-layer model across Strategy, Scope, Structure, Skeleton, and Surface. Where the gap between expected task difficulty and actual experience was largest, that is where we focused, measured with SUS and NPS.

chart, swap in laterexpected task difficulty vs experience, with sus and nps
chart, swap in laterkano model, feature prioritisation
  1. finding 01

    Trust but verify was the dominant mindset

    Marketers were optimistic about AI but needed full transparency at every approval stage. This validated the reasoning card in the process pane.

  2. finding 02

    Terminology confusion created friction

    Users did not share the system's mental model. This drove a terminology audit across the platform.

  3. finding 03

    Verification gaps were the biggest barrier to adoption

    This became a priority for the action bar design and the change-differentiation model.

notebooklm

Usability transcripts and research notes fed into a shared intelligence layer that PMs, engineering, and design could all draw from independently.

kano testing

Prioritized the agentic MVP. Must-haves shipped; everything else went to backlog with a user-grounded rationale.

08 takeaways

What building a product that did not exist taught me

  1. t1

    Scale is a decision you make early

    The pattern language and IA were not polish. They were the foundation everything else was built on.

  2. t2

    Agentic UX is about trust, not intelligence

    Marketers did not need the AI to be smarter. They needed it to be legible.

  3. t3

    Patterns over screens

    Non-deterministic workflows do not scale screen by screen. A pattern language gives every new workflow a foundation to build on.

  4. t4

    Ambiguity is the real brief

    We were designing while the architecture was still being figured out. Keeping the team aligned through constant change was as important as the design work.

  5. t5

    Collaboration is everything

    This project taught me how to move fast across functions without losing coherence.

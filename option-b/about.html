About

Hi, I'm Raashi.

Product Designer at Intuit, via Accenture Song, based in Bengaluru. I design across enterprise software, AI tooling, and B2B SaaS. Drawn to the new, the undefined, and the problems worth figuring out.

My story

From industrial design to agentic AI

I studied Industrial Design at MIT Institute of Design, a discipline that trains you to think about the whole object: how it's made, how it's held, where it sits in someone's life. I never really left that lens behind. I just moved it from physical products to digital ones.

Today I'm a core designer on Marketing Studio, Intuit's agentic AI platform. This work took campaign execution from 1 to 2 weeks across 10+ tools and 5+ teams down to under 20 minutes. Along the way I founded its design system, authored a platform-wide pattern language for agentic UX, and built a Figma-to-code convergence workflow with Claude. The through-line from industrial design is still there: I want to understand the whole system before I design any single part of it.

How I think

I design for the whole ecosystem

I like knowing where things come from and where they go after they're made on my product, and how that shapes the experience. A campaign, a file, an artifact rarely lives in one place. It gets created, edited, cascaded, approved, rolled back. If I only design the screen where it's made, I've designed half the problem.

That's why my strongest work sits at the seams: version history and change propagation for live, agent-generated campaigns; a pattern language that keeps behavior consistent across every module; a design system that closes the gap between Figma and production code. The interesting problems are usually the ones about how the parts connect, not the parts themselves.

How I work
01

Map the ecosystem first

Before designing a screen, I trace where things come from and where they go next. Half the problem lives outside the surface I'm handed.

02

Find the structure in the ambiguity

New, undefined problems don't come with a brief. I look for the underlying pattern, the one job dressed up as five, the shared model beneath the variations.

03

Design for real state, not the demo

Especially with AI: things branch, fail partway, and produce live artifacts people need to change safely. I design for failure modes and non-determinism, not just the happy path.

04

Validate with real usage

I ground decisions in discovery sessions and research (Kano, SUS, NPS), and I ship runnable prototypes so stakeholders can feel the behavior before sign-off.

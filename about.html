My story

From industrial design to agentic AI

I studied Industrial Design at MIT Institute of Design. The discipline trains you to think about the whole object: how it's made, how it's held, where it sits in someone's life. I never left that lens behind. I just moved it from physical products to digital ones.

Today I'm a core designer on Marketing Studio, Intuit's agentic AI platform, work that collapsed campaign execution from two weeks to twenty minutes. Along the way I founded its design system and wrote the pattern language for its agentic UX.

The through-line is still industrial design: understand the whole system before designing any part of it.

How I think

I design for the whole ecosystem

I like knowing where things come from and where they go after they leave the screen I'm designing. A campaign, a file, an artifact rarely lives in one place. It gets created, edited, cascaded, approved, rolled back. Design only the screen where it's made, and you've designed half the problem.

That's why my strongest work sits at the seams: change propagation for live campaigns, a pattern language that holds behavior consistent across modules, a design system that closes the Figma-to-code gap. The problems worth having are about how the parts connect, not the parts themselves.

How I work

Four things I keep coming back to

  1. 01

    Map the ecosystem first

    Before designing a screen, I trace where things come from and where they go next. Half the problem lives outside the surface I'm handed.

  2. 02

    Find the structure in the ambiguity

    New, undefined problems don't come with a brief. I look for the underlying pattern, the one job dressed up as five, the shared model beneath the variations.

  3. 03

    Design for real state, not the demo

    Especially with AI: things branch, fail partway, and produce live artifacts people need to change safely. I design for failure modes and non-determinism, not just the happy path.

  4. 04

    Validate with real usage

    I ground decisions in discovery sessions and research (Kano, SUS, NPS), and I ship runnable prototypes so stakeholders can feel the behavior before sign-off.
